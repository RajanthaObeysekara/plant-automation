02

Power budget

Worst realistic case is still one pump + its own valve energized at once — the firmware's interlocks (ActuatorController) never run two lines simultaneously, and never open a valve without its pump. The inlet valve is the one exception: auto-fill can legitimately overlap a mist cycle (different lines entirely), adding a fixed ~0.5A rather than compounding the pump/valve worst case. Either way, you're well inside the SMPS's 5A rating.

Wire gauge: use at least 18 AWG for anything carrying pump current (the 12V runs to the pumps/valves and the SMPS-to-relay-COM run); 22–24 AWG is fine for every logic/signal wire (GPIO lines, I2C, sensor VCC).

05

Before you power it on

Five things worth checking with a multimeter first

All cheap and all easy to get backwards on a first build.
  • Relay VCC vs COM are two different rails.VCC (logic) is 5V from the buck converter; COM (switched) is 12V straight from the bus. Wiring the pump's 12V into the logic VCC pin will kill the relay board.
  • Common ground, always.ESP32 GND, relay logic GND, and the 12V supply's ground all need to be tied together — otherwise the ESP32's GPIO signal has no shared reference and the relay won't trigger reliably, if at all.
  • Confirm active-low vs active-high on the relay board before flashing.Most cheap boards energize on a LOW signal. The firmware's RELAY_ACTIVE_LOW flag in Config.h must match your actual board, or every relay will be inverted — pumps running when they should be off.
  • Fuse goes right after the SMPS, before anything else.It protects the whole 12V bus, not just one device — put it anywhere else and part of the circuit is unprotected.
  • Power up logic before actuators.Get the ESP32 booting and the OLED showing readings first, with the pump/valve wires disconnected. Only connect the 12V actuator wiring once you've confirmed the relay clicks on command — much easier to debug a GPIO issue without a pump also running.
  • Physically double-check the fungicide line never tees into the water/feed manifold.This is a plumbing mistake, not a wiring one — the firmware has no way to detect it. If it's plumbed wrong, the first automated dose will run wettable-powder residue through your 30-µm nozzles.
  • Test the auto-fill timeout before trusting it unattended.Temporarily cover the full sensor so it never triggers, and confirm the inlet valve actually closes on its own after AUTOFILL_MAX_SECONDS (10 minutes by default) — that's the only thing standing between a sensor failure and an overflowing tank.
