Assumptions — read this first

This is a sized template, not a survey of your actual land

I have no data on your plot's real dimensions, orientation, existing structures, or soil — none of that has been shared, and I'm not going to invent specifics about your property and present them as fact. What follows is a realistic, buildable reference size for the next phase (a smallholder commercial orchid house), scaled from your current 10-pot setup and general good practice for Kadawatha's climate. Treat every measurement below as a starting point to check against your actual site, not a survey result.
  • Footprint used here: 6 m × 12 m (72 m²).A common size for a smallholder net-house in Sri Lanka — big enough to be a real step up from a bench, small enough to self-build and fund incrementally. Modular in 6 m bays, so it extends to 6×18 m, 6×24 m, etc. if your land allows.
  • Climate design target: Kadawatha's wet-zone tropics — 70–85% RH, 26–32°C, monsoon rain from both the SW (May–Sep) and NE (Dec–Feb).This drives the "rain-break roof + open shade-net walls" structure below rather than a sealed glasshouse, which would overheat in this climate.
  • You'll need to confirm: actual plot dimensions and shape, water source (mains/well/rainwater), electrical supply capacity, and whether the site is level or needs grading.See Open questions — these change the design meaningfully and only you can answer them.
02

Structure

Epiphytic orchids want filtered light and constant airflow, not a sealed enclosure — the same logic already in the care plan (60–70% shade net, clear roof only to break monsoon rain). The structure below is a shade-net house with a rain-break roof, not a conventional glasshouse: solid overhead, open on the sides.

ElementSpecWhy
FrameGalvanized steel (GI) pipe, concrete footingsCorrosion-resistant in high humidity; footings resist monsoon wind uplift
Height2.4 m eaves / 3.2 m ridgeStack ventilation — hot humid air rises and escapes at the ridge instead of pooling on the plants
Roof200-micron UV-stabilized clear PE film or corrugated polycarbonate, pitched ≥15°Breaks direct monsoon rain (crown-rot risk) while staying translucent; pitch sheds water fast
Walls50–70% green shade net, full height, roll-up sidesMatches the existing shading spec; open sides keep air moving — still and humid is exactly what fungal disease wants
GuttersPVC gutter along both eaves to a first-flush diverterRoof becomes your rainwater harvesting catchment — free, soft water for a plain-water tank
PerimeterLow block wall (~30 cm) under the netKeeps splash-back soil/debris off the lowest bench row

Floor plan

Three raised benches running the length of the house, wide aisles for a wheelbarrow or trolley, and a utility bay at the entrance holding every tank and the control panel in one serviceable spot.

Bench specValue
Height75 cm (waist height, standing access)
SurfaceGalvanized weld-mesh or slatted PVC on a steel frame — open surface for airflow and drainage under the pots
Width1 m (reachable from both sides)
Pot spacing~18 cm centers — enough gap between pots for air movement; tighter spacing is exactly how crown rot spreads bench-to-bench
04

Auto-fertigation — mixing, not just dosing

The current design (see the main architecture doc) has the grower pre-mix each week's fertilizer by hand into a single feed tank. This version automates the mixing step itself: two concentrated stock tanks, a small batch mixing tank, and dosing pumps that measure the correct stock volume in on the day it's due — nobody has to remember to mix a jug.

05

Dosing sequence — what happens on a feed day

StepActionChannel(s)
1Fill the mixing tank with a measured volume of plain waterCH3
2Run the correct stock pump for a calculated duration — Basfoliar in weeks 1–2, Mugasole in weeks 3–4, neither in week 5 (flush)CH4 or CH5
3Recirculate briefly to homogenize the batch (optional but recommended)CH8
4Draw the mixed batch out to the shared manifold and sprayCH6, CH7
5Refill the mixing tank with a small plain-water rinse and draw that through tooCH3, then CH6/CH7

Dose duration is just flow-rate math: a peristaltic dosing pump rated at, say, 100 mL/min needs desiredStockMl ÷ 100 × 60 seconds of runtime. Scale the 0.25 g/250 mL hand-mix ratio up to whatever batch volume the mixing tank holds — the ratio doesn't change, only the total volume. The rinse step exists so the next scheduled plain-water mist doesn't carry yesterday's fertilizer residue through the same nozzles, and so Basfoliar and Mugasole never sit mixed together in the tank between rotation phases.

Controls & wiring changes

This adds four channels to the 4-channel design in the main architecture doc — plan for an 8-channel relay setup (two 4-channel DC-rated boards, or one 8-channel board) rather than the single 4-channel board currently in the cart.

ChannelDrivesStatus
CH1 / CH2Water pump / valve — direct daily misting linealready planned
CH3Mixing-tank fill valvenew
CH4Dosing pump A — Basfoliar P-40 stocknew
CH5Dosing pump B — Mugasole Treble 20 stocknew
CH6 / CH7Mixing-tank draw pump / valve — feeds the manifoldnew
CH8Agitation / recirculation pump (optional)new, optional

On the firmware side, this is the same non-blocking state-machine pattern already built for the mist/feed cycle (see firmware/esp32-unit/src/main.cpp) — it just grows two more states: FILLING_MIX and DOSING, inserted before the existing FEEDING state, with the stock pump and duration chosen by the same week-of-rotation logic already in RuleEngine.

Build phasing

Phase A — now

Finish the 10-pot bench exactly as already designed: DC relay swap, real soil— sorry, canopy sensor mounting, dosing pump for a single hand-mixed feed tank.

no structure yet — proves the control logic

Phase B — structure

Build the shade-net house and benches. Move the existing 10 pots in. Still one hand-mixed feed tank — the structure is the only new variable.

validates the physical space before automating dosing

Phase C — auto-fertigation

Add the stock tanks, mixing tank, and extra relay channels from this document. Only once misting is already boring and reliable.

the riskiest phase — most new hardware, most new firmware states

Phase D — scale out

Add units per the greenhouse fleet architecture (one ESP32 per room/bench group), fill more of the bench capacity, extend the house in 6 m bays if the land allows.

same backend, same firmware — just more units
09

Open questions — only you can answer these

Before any of this gets built

The dimensions and equipment list above are a template. These are the specifics I have no way to know:
  • Actual plot dimensions and shape.Determines whether 6×12 m fits at all, and how the modular 6 m bays should orient.
  • Water source.Rainwater harvest alone may not cover dry spells — is there a well or mains connection to back it up?
  • Electrical supply.Five pumps plus a relay controller need a real circuit, not an extension cord from the house.
  • Site slope and soil.Affects footings and whether the floor needs grading or a hardstanding for the benches.
  • Budget and timeline.Changes whether Phase C (auto-fertigation) is worth building at all versus staying with hand-mixed feeding indefinitely.
